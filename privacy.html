Privacy & Data

How this site handles data

Two things matter here: what this site knows about you as a visitor, and where the property records on the map come from. Both are covered below.

Who runs this site

This is an independent project and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to the State of Ohio, the Ohio Department of Taxation, or any county auditor or government agency. It is built entirely from records those offices publish for the public.

If you have a question about your own property taxes or credits, contact your county auditor directly. This site cannot change your tax bill, and nothing here is legal or tax advice.

What this site knows about its visitors

There are no accounts, no forms, no cookies, and no ad trackers. Nothing you do here is tied to you as a person. The fonts are served from this site itself, so your visit is not shared with a font provider.

The only measurement is Cloudflare Web Analytics, a cookie-free counter. It tells us totals, like how many people viewed a page and which countries they came from. It does not use cookies, does not follow you to other sites, and does not build a profile of you.

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub records standard server logs (such as IP addresses) to run the service, under GitHub's privacy statement. We never see those logs.

Where the property data comes from

Every name, address, parcel number, and dollar figure on this site comes from public records published by Ohio county auditors: their bulk data downloads, GIS services, and property search sites. Property ownership and tax records are public under Ohio law.

Nothing here comes from private databases, purchased lists, or scraping of personal accounts. The figures reflect tax year 2025 county files and are refreshed when counties publish new data, so recent sales and corrections can take time to appear.

A flag is a lead, not an accusation

This site flags patterns that are worth checking, not proof of wrongdoing. A home can appear here for innocent reasons: the credit always runs a year behind, so a home that just sold may still show the previous owner's credit; two different people can share a name; and a trust, LLC, or life estate can legally hold the credit with the right paperwork on file.

Anyone using this data should verify each parcel on the county auditor's website before drawing conclusions or contacting anyone.

Corrections and removal requests

If you are listed here and believe the match is wrong, or you have paperwork on file that makes a flagged credit legitimate, email ohiocreditaudit@gmail.com with the county and parcel number. We will review it and correct or annotate the entry in the next data update.

Because the underlying records are public county data, removing an entry here does not remove it from the county's own records.
